Offset and the Green Book

The UK Offset Policy says the Offset Authority will value Offset with a framework consistent with the principles of HM Treasury's Green Book. Its detailed guidance is still to come. Until then, this page explains what the Green Book is and puts its principles into the terms Offset managers already use.

Read the Green Book on GOV.UKThe UK Offset Policy in brief

What the Green Book is

The Green Book is HM Treasury's guidance on appraisal: how government assesses the costs, benefits and risks of different ways of meeting its objectives. It is mandatory for central government departments and their arm's-length bodies. HM Treasury published the 2026 edition in February 2026.

It asks one question of every proposal: how much better off is the UK as a whole, compared with what would have happened anyway?

Why it matters for Offset

Section 3.11.1 of the policy says the Offset Authority will use a transparent value framework consistent with the Green Book's principles. The policy defines UK value added as value genuinely generated in the UK that can be evidenced.

So the face value of a contract, an investment or a licence is the starting point, not the credit. Multipliers then add extra credit for priority places, activities and sectors. Their values are still to be published.

The Green Book in Offset terms

Each line pairs a familiar Offset term with the Green Book idea behind it.

Additionality
Green Book: additionalityCredit only for value that would not exist without the Offset obligation.
Incrementality
Green Book: deadweightBusiness that would have happened anyway, such as existing contracts and routine re-orders, earns nothing.
Baseline
Green Book: business as usualThe starting position that new business is measured against.
Causality
Green Book: outcomes that happen as a result of the interventionThe supplier must show that its obligation caused the work.
UK content
Green Book: leakageOnly value made in the UK counts; imported content is deducted.
No re-badging
Green Book: displacement and substitutionMoving work between UK firms, or relabelling existing work, adds nothing.
Sustainability
Green Book: durability and discountingCapability that lasts for years counts for more than one-off work.
Credit on delivery
Green Book: optimism biasCredit is for what is delivered and checked, not what is promised.
Activity, not payments
Green Book: transfersA payment on its own is not value; credit follows the UK activity it pays for.
Capability building
Green Book: supply-side benefitsCredit follows lasting gains in skills, technology and productivity, not turnover.
Regional and SME multipliers
Green Book: place-based and distributional analysisBenefits in less productive regions and in smaller firms carry extra weight.
Strategic fit
Green Book: the strategic caseProjects should serve the published priority areas.
Verification
Green Book: monitoring and evaluationEvidence of delivery is checked before credit is granted.

What this means in practice

  • Real UK makers with high UK content count for more than distributors and importers.
  • New capability, such as technology transfer, research and skills, counts for more than more of the same.
  • New work for established UK companies on what they already make still counts as UK value, even where it attracts no multiplier.
  • Plan the evidence from day one: the starting position, the UK content, new jobs and who fills them, approvals and exports.

A plain-English reading, not the Offset Authority's guidance. The Offset Authority will publish its own valuation guidance. The policy and the Green Book on GOV.UK are the authority.
