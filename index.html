UK National Offset Council

An informal gathering in central London for people with an interest in UK Offset and the UK Offset Policy: officials from the Ministry of Defence and other government departments, UK companies and overseas suppliers. Each meeting is half a day, free to attend, and brings industry and government together on how Offset works in practice.

Meetings are held quarterly whenever possible.

Why UK-NOC, and why now

The Ministry of Defence published the UK Offset Policy in October 2026, applying Offset to large defence purchases from abroad. Overseas suppliers, their UK partners and government all gain from a shared, practical understanding of how commitments are set, met and credited.

Many overseas companies already manage Offset obligations in other countries, and many UK companies already supply them. UK-NOC gives both a regular place to compare experience, raise practical problems early and understand the thinking behind the rules.

It is deliberately informal: half a day of open discussion and networking, not a conference or a sales event.

Between meetings, this site shares the Ministry of Defence's public announcements on UK Offset as they appear on GOV.UK, and a weekly round-up of Offset news from around the world.

Who it is for

Anyone with a working interest in UK Offset is welcome.

The Ministry of Defence and government

Officials responsible for UK Offset policy and defence procurement, invited to set out policy and hear industry's experience.

UK companies

UK companies of every size, from primes to SMEs, that partner with overseas suppliers or want to.

Overseas suppliers

Overseas defence companies with UK Offset commitments, or expecting them on future UK contracts, and their UK subsidiaries.

What the Council discusses

Three themes run through every meeting.

Macroeconomic rules
How commitments are set and valued, what counts towards them, how they are reported, and the economic goals behind them.
National security requirements
Sovereign capability, security of supply, ownership, protection of intellectual property and export control.
Supply contract obligations
How commitments are written into contracts, flow down to UK suppliers, and are delivered and evidenced over the life of a programme.

How it works

When
Quarterly whenever possible, for half a day.
Where
Central London, at the offices of an organisation that lends a meeting room.
Cost
Free. Attendees cover their own travel and expenses.
Format
Informal: open discussion, then time to meet people.
Ground rules
Policy and general practice only. Live competitions, individual bids and prices are not discussed.
Chatham House Rule
All meetings are held under the Chatham House Rule. You may use what you hear, but you may not reveal who said it or which organisation they represent. This allows everyone to speak freely.
Organiser
Stephen Lewis organises each meeting. UK-NOC is independent: it is not a government body and does not speak for the Ministry of Defence.
