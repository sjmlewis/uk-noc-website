The UK Offset Policy in brief

On 30 October 2026 the Ministry of Defence published the UK Offset Policy. An overseas supplier that wins a large UK defence contract commits to deliver UK value equal to the contract, through projects agreed with a new Offset Authority. These are the main points.

Read the policy on GOV.UKOffset and the Green Book

The main points

A summary, not legal advice. The policy on GOV.UK is the authority. Further guidance, including details of the multipliers, is due to be published.

When
Published on 30 October 2026. The Ministry of Defence will publish more detail before it applies Offset to defence and security procurements in 2027.
Who
Overseas suppliers of defence or national security materiel, systems, services, support, upgrades and life-cycle activity. It may also apply where a supplier is registered in the UK but a significant part of the contract value comes from abroad.
Threshold
Contracts worth £50 million or more. Related acquisitions may be added together over up to five years.
Obligation
Normally 100% of the contract value, in a separate Offset Agreement. The main contract is not signed until the Offset Agreement is in place. Projects are then agreed with the Offset Authority.
Timescale
Normally up to ten years, with milestones of 30% by the end of Year 3, 50% by Year 5 and 100% by Year 10. The Offset Authority may vary these.
What counts
Direct Offset, tied to what is being bought, and indirect Offset, which supports the wider UK defence and security sector: contracts with UK businesses, technology and know-how transfer, research and development with UK universities and industry, investment in UK facilities, maintenance and sustainment capability, training and apprenticeships, and help for UK partners to export.
Approval
A project is eligible for credit only if the Offset Authority approves it in writing before it starts. Offset activity must be additional to ordinary business.
Value
The Offset Authority values projects with a transparent framework consistent with HM Treasury's Green Book principles. How the Green Book shapes value.
Multipliers
Extra credit for priority activities, such as strengthening regional clusters and supply chains, developing frontier technologies, apprenticeships and critical skills, opportunities for SMEs and mid-tier firms, and sovereign capability. Details are to be published.
Credits
Normally awarded in arrears, after delivery and verification. Suppliers report by 31 March each year. Banked credit normally expires after five years.
Remedies
Compensation for a missed milestone is normally at least 10% of the value not delivered at that milestone. Penalties are a last resort, for suppliers that do not engage with the policy.
Transparency
The Offset Authority will keep a public list of suppliers with Offset Agreements and the value each still has to deliver.

What it means

For overseas suppliers

Plan early. You will need UK partners that can make your parts now, and partners that could with your support, with evidence of where and how they work in the UK.

For UK companies

The policy opens supply chains to UK primes, mid-tier companies, SMEs, universities and research organisations. Overseas suppliers will be looking for capable UK partners.

Talk it through

Discuss the policy with others working on it at UK-NOC meetings.
